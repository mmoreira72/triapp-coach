## Key question 1 — What IF / VI / power targets do the articles give per race distance and athlete level?

### Takeaway
The Science category defines IF and VI as the two race-file metrics that describe "specific endurance" and "how evenly paced the effort was", and tells coaches to set target numbers per athlete and course, but it never publishes distance-specific IF/VI ranges. The concrete ranges (AG full-distance bike IF 0.65–0.75, pro 0.78–0.82; half-distance IF 0.83–0.87 per Allen & Coggan; VI ≤ 1.05 for any triathlon bike leg; marathon run at 0.91–0.95 of threshold pace) live in other TrainingPeaks articles.

### Cited Findings

**From the Science category (primary)**
- [Triathlon-specific] Alan Couzens names three race-file metrics: Efficiency Factor (NP ÷ HR) as "an indicator of basic aerobic fitness", Intensity Factor as "an indicator of specific endurance" ("IRONMAN athletes have different physiological strengths than athletes who specialize in sprint events… expressed by the athlete's IF and how it changes for the performance of different durations"), and Variability Index as "an indicator of how evenly paced the effort was" — [3 Key Metrics of Effective Athlete Data Analysis for Coaches](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/3-key-metrics-of-effective-data-analysis-for-coaches/)
- [Triathlon-specific] "Athletes hoping to do well in long-duration events will need the ability to hold high percentages of their aerobic maximum for long periods of time, i.e. to have a relatively high IF", tracked via the Mean Maximal Power Curve — [3 Key Metrics](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/3-key-metrics-of-effective-data-analysis-for-coaches/)
- [General endurance] "There is a unique, optimal power distribution for every athlete and every course. The VI number helps us to quantify that and to test different distribution strategies… If we decide that the optimal race strategy is for an athlete to hit a particular climb just below FTP, there had better be some climbing at this intensity in the training" — [3 Key Metrics](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/3-key-metrics-of-effective-data-analysis-for-coaches/)
- [General endurance] Normalized Power algorithm: 30-s rolling average → 4th power → mean → 4th root; the example file (400 W 70 s, 600 W 30 s, 400 W 20 s) gives AP 450 W / NP 478 W (VI 1.06) — [Understanding Normalized Power Calculations](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/normalized-power-how-coaches-use/)
- [General endurance] "At TrainingPeaks, coaches are taught to dismiss NP values for durations under 10 minutes… The 30-second window should be less than or equal to 5% of the overall interval." NP "should not… judge the difficulty of short periods within races." "Average power is more appropriate than NP on long climbs or during time trials. NP should not be used as a measure of judging performance." "NP may underestimate the load on an athlete new to the sport but overestimate the load on a very well-trained athlete." — [Understanding Normalized Power Calculations](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/normalized-power-how-coaches-use/)
- [General endurance] Marathon-specific IF for running (the only distance-specific intensity figure found in the category): coach Andrew Simmons "normally use[s] pace zones of .91 to .95 for marathon training and this is what I set in the workout builder to achieve the 'predicted' results" — [3 Things Your Athletes' Running Data Could Be Telling You](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/athletes-running-data/)
- [General endurance] Marathon-prep run example: long run 23.6 mi with 2 × 25 min at ~92–94% of (running) FTP, 5 min easy between — [Using WKO4 to Analyze the Struggle in a Run Workout](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/using-wko4-to-analyze-the-struggle-in-a-run-workout/)
- [Triathlon-specific] Modelling assumption for an age-group middle-distance bike: CdA 0.30, 35 km/h → ~200 W; a 70.3 course "typical spread of speeds" is ~18–26 mph; a skinsuit ≈ 5% CdA reduction (0.40→0.38), a TT bike ≈ 25% (0.38→0.29) — [What's More Important for Cycling — Aerodynamics or Power?](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/whats-important-aerodynamics-power/)
- [Triathlon-specific] "Less power is not always worse, provided it's offset with improved aerodynamics… more energy left for the run… If a more aerodynamic position fatigues muscles relied upon for running, it might offset any gains" — [Aerodynamics or Power?](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/whats-important-aerodynamics-power/)
- [General endurance] Athlete phenotype from the 90-day MMP curve: "Time Trialist: a flatter curve with strong long-duration power and weaker short-duration output, suited to time trials, long-course triathlon, or ultra-endurance racing"; 90 days is "generally accepted as a valid period to judge an athlete's current abilities without requiring new tests"; energy-system bands: 1 s–15/30 s ATP-PCr, 30 s–4/8 min glycolytic, > 8 min aerobic — [All About the Mean Max Power Curve](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/all-about-the-mean-max-power-curve/)
- [General endurance] FTP defined as "the highest average power that a rider can maintain during 40 to 60 minutes… a good predictor for performance in a long time trial"; critical periods: short 5 s–1 min, medium 1–10 min (typically 4–5 min), long > 10 min up to 1 h — [Should You Be Evaluating Your Cyclists Using Critical Periods?](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/should-you-be-evaluating-your-cyclists-using-critical-periods/)
- [Triathlon-specific] "Long-course triathletes and Ironman athletes often do better with a lower VLa Max, because higher lactate production can reduce the fraction of VO₂max they can sustain at LT2" — [A Coach's Guide to Lactate Testing](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/lactate-threshold-testing-better-performance/)
- [Triathlon-specific] Off-the-bike run pacing by power: "those who struggle [running off the bike] are doing so because of a reduced efficiency factor, but their power values remain largely unaffected. Therefore, racing triathlon becomes a simple case of targeting a power number based on what they can sustain for the duration of the race." — [Is Running with Power Worth It?](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/is-running-with-power-worth-it/)

**Outside the Science category (TrainingPeaks, secondary)**
- [Triathlon-specific] Full-distance bike: "For the pro men and women, we typically see an IF of .78 to .82… Age group athletes should aim for an IF of .65 to .75 in order to set themselves up for a strong run… If it is your first IRONMAN or you are not a strong runner, lower your IF to .65." Example: FTP 250 W × 0.70 = 175 W "for a moderate to aggressive bike ride" — [Lessons from Kona (AJ Johnson)](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/lessons-from-kona-how-to-nail-an-ironman-bike/)
- [Triathlon-specific] Full-distance VI: "For every rider… a VI of 1.05 or lower is the goal… every file from the pro athletes does have a VI of 1.05 or lower"; Kona AG course-record holder Kyle Buckingham VI 1.02, Faris Al Sultan 1.04; pro women 3.1–3.6 W/kg on the bike — [Lessons from Kona](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/lessons-from-kona-how-to-nail-an-ironman-bike/)
- [Triathlon-specific] Half-distance bike: "According to Hunter Allen and Andrew Coggan… the optimal Intensity Factor range to race a Half Ironman is .83–.87"; the author (40 × 70.3 finisher) has ranged 0.76–0.87 and VI 1.00–1.08; at the 2014 70.3 Worlds he rode IF 0.82 / VI 1.02, pushed the last 7.5 km hard, then "could not match the leg speed" on the run — "I had used a couple of extra bullets" — [Taking Risks: IRONMAN 70.3 WC Bike Pacing](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/taking-risks-ironman-70-3-world-championships-bike-pacing/)
- [Triathlon-specific] "Joe Friel has stated that an optimal VI for triathlons is 1.05 or below in order to run well off the bike" — [Taking Risks](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/taking-risks-ironman-70-3-world-championships-bike-pacing/); "For a steadily paced ride such as a time trial or triathlon bike leg… a VI of 1.05 or less… criteriums and mountain bike races… VI of 1.3 or higher" — [Intro to TP Metrics Part Two (Schell)](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/an-introduction-to-more-trainingpeaks-metrics-part-two/)

### Inferences
- A rule set for the assistant that is consistent with all TrainingPeaks sources: full-distance bike IF 0.65–0.75 (0.65 first-timer/weak runner, 0.70 moderate-aggressive, 0.75 strong runner), pro 0.78–0.82; half-distance 0.76–0.87 (0.83–0.87 is the Allen/Coggan "optimal", the lower end for weaker runners); VI ≤ 1.05 for any non-draft triathlon bike, with 1.02–1.04 what the best files show; marathon-type runs at 0.91–0.95 of threshold pace. Flag these as coming from TrainingPeaks generally, not the Science category.
- Because NP is unreliable under 10 min and the 30-s window must be ≤ 5% of the segment, a race-diagnosis tool should compute VI/IF only on segments ≥ 10 min (ideally whole legs or ≥ 10-min course sections) and use average power for climbs.
- The Science category's explicit "optimal VI is course-specific" stance (Couzens) plus Epton's physics (below) mean VI ≤ 1.05 is a ceiling, not an instruction to ride constant power on hilly courses.

### Gaps
- No Science-category article gives IF or VI ranges for sprint or Olympic-distance triathlon, nor pro-vs-AG ranges for 70.3; no TrainingPeaks source fetched gives numeric IF targets for sprint/Olympic bike legs.
- No article gives run IF targets for the triathlon run (off the bike) by distance; Simmons's 0.91–0.95 is for open marathons.
- No article gives target run "walking share" or expected pace fade percentages for triathlon runs.

## Key question 2 — How do they define and measure durability, and which training builds it?

### Takeaway
Durability is defined as "the time and degree of performance deterioration caused by fatigue during prolonged exercise" (Rowe) or "the ability to keep producing the same output at a similar physiological cost late in a race" (Pittman). It is measured by fresh-vs-fatigued repeat tests after a fixed kJ dose (e.g., 5-min power before and after 2,000 kJ; 10% drop in the worked example) and monitored via Pa:HR/Pw:HR decoupling (≤ 5% is "a happy place"). It is built mainly by years of accumulated volume, high Zone-2 volume, back-to-back long sessions ("mini-blocks"), and fuelling; the category does not give a specific "power after X kJ" benchmark for triathletes.

### Cited Findings

**Definition**
- [General endurance] "Durability is the time and degree of performance deterioration caused by fatigue during prolonged exercise… hours into the ride, maintaining 200 watts requires higher oxygen consumption and, thus, more energy (kJ)… a rider's durability in longer endurance events (~90 min+) is a key performance indicator, distinguishing higher-performing athletes from their less-successful peers." — [Predicting and Measuring Kilojoules to Determine Athlete Durability](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/predicting-measuring-kilojoules-trainingpeaks/)
- [General endurance] "Durability, often referred to as fatigue resistance, describes an athlete's ability to maintain a high fraction of their aerobic capacity and movement economy as fatigue accumulates over long durations… In shorter events such as the 5K through the marathon, performance is largely determined by VO2max, lactate threshold (or critical speed), and running economy… as race duration increases… the outcome is often dictated not by how high that ceiling is, but by how much of it the athlete can still access after several hours." — [How to Build Fatigue Resistance for Ultras](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/fatigue-resistance-ultras-molly-seidel-training/)
- [General endurance] kJ ≈ kcal (1:1) because the 4.184 kJ/kcal factor and 20–25% gross efficiency (~22.5%) cancel; example 500 kJ → 531 kcal — [Predicting and Measuring Kilojoules](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/predicting-measuring-kilojoules-trainingpeaks/). "Only ~20% of the calories you burn go toward muscular power… when you pedal at 200 watts, you're also creating 800 watts of heat" — [Analyze 360](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/see-and-analyze-every-metric/)

**Measurement**
- [General endurance] Field protocol: "test their 'fresh' (few kJs burned) abilities across race-relevant durations, perform work (burn kJs) to accumulate fatigue, and retest… For example, a cyclist could perform a 5-minute maximal interval after a brief warm-up, ride until they burn 2,000 kJs, and then perform a 5-minute interval again… First 5-minute Average Watts – 300; Second 5-minute Average Watts – 270; Result – 10% reduction in 5-minute power after 2,000 kJ" — [Predicting and Measuring Kilojoules](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/predicting-measuring-kilojoules-trainingpeaks/)
- [General endurance] In-TrainingPeaks workflow: build a Workout Builder session with a test block (1, 5, 10 min…), then "interval blocks that necessitate significant energy expenditure (1,000 kJ+)", repeat the test block, lap it, tag it, re-prescribe periodically; "overall kJ expenditure loosely predicts durability" (Kilojoules-by-Week chart); "approximating the kJ demands of different events can inform training prescriptions" — [Predicting and Measuring Kilojoules](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/predicting-measuring-kilojoules-trainingpeaks/)
- [General endurance] "There isn't a clear consensus on a durability testing protocol"; lab predictors are VO2max, gross efficiency and fat oxidation — [Predicting and Measuring Kilojoules](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/predicting-measuring-kilojoules-trainingpeaks/)
- [General endurance] Decoupling as durability monitor: "We also closely monitor Pa:HR to assess cardiac drift, which offers insight into durability, heat stress, and fueling or hydration issues… improved durability shows up as the ability to complete more work at the same perceived effort with less physiological drift and better late-run stability"; Normalized Graded Pace used for elevation; IF as a "high-level check" of session demand vs current threshold — [How to Build Fatigue Resistance for Ultras](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/fatigue-resistance-ultras-molly-seidel-training/)
- [General endurance] "I look to decoupling via PA:HR. Within the 0-5% range, we're in a happy place… To achieve a positive [decoupling] two things must happen: 1) HR must drift upwards while pace maintains or 2) HR maintains and pace declines. An example could be an athlete who runs a 5K test and goes out too hard, then fades in the final 1000-meters while their HR is pegged." Worked example: 8.87% decoupling in a 4-min ramp-up vs 1.47% over the remaining 8 min — [3 Things Your Athletes' Running Data Could Be Telling You](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/athletes-running-data/)
- [General endurance] Fatigue diagnostics in a long run with power (WKO4 first-half vs second-half report): power +4.6%, speed +10%, Running Effectiveness +6.1%, power:GCT ratio ("a good marker of fatigue") +5.8%, GCT and vertical oscillation down — despite high RPE, "the production was still there"; "somewhere in that last 10K of a marathon, we want to be able to dig deep but still produce power and speed" — [Using WKO4 to Analyze the Struggle in a Run Workout](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/using-wko4-to-analyze-the-struggle-in-a-run-workout/)
- [General endurance] Running-economy-under-fatigue check: "A runner who requires more power to reach the same speed is less efficient"; if power drops but pace holds, "no material loss in running economy under fatigue" — [Is Coaching With Running Power Better Than Pace or HR?](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/coaching-running-power-better/). Speed (m/s) = Running Effectiveness (kg/N) × Power (W/kg) (Palladino) — [Is Running with Power Worth It?](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/is-running-with-power-worth-it/)
- [General endurance] Form erosion inflates power-based run TSS: "if… the athlete's form erodes during raceday, there is a good likelihood that the TSS value will end up higher than anticipated" — [Is Running with Power Worth It?](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/is-running-with-power-worth-it/)
- [General endurance] Lactate-curve diagnostic: "if LT2 looks solid but longer steady work fades, you may be looking at pacing, fueling, and fatigue resistance more than raw physiology"; if the athlete produces big short numbers but "falls apart… near LT2, that usually points to an aerobic base or threshold durability issue" — [A Coach's Guide to Lactate Testing](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/lactate-threshold-testing-better-performance/)
- [General endurance] Breathing-rate vs minute-ventilation decoupling as a fatigue signal in long low-intensity sessions (Tymewear) — [Analyze 360](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/see-and-analyze-every-metric/)

**Training that builds durability**
- [General endurance] "researchers have found that durability can be improved through training and that it improves as younger athletes progress into older athletes… some relationship between durability and time spent… below aerobic threshold (VT1/LT1) and… polarized [distribution]; however, the strongest predictor is how much and how long the athlete has been training over the years" — [Predicting and Measuring Kilojoules](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/predicting-measuring-kilojoules-trainingpeaks/)
- [General endurance] Nutrition: a durable athlete can "use a higher proportion of fat as fuel at higher exercise intensities" and "ingest (and absorb) a lot of carbohydrates (30-120g/hr depending on intensity, duration, and workout/race goal)" — [Predicting and Measuring Kilojoules](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/predicting-measuring-kilojoules-trainingpeaks/)
- [General endurance] Molly Seidel's ultra build: "high volume of Zone 2 aerobic running, supplemented with controlled doses of Zone 3 work that are layered into long runs at race-relevant effort"; volume up, intensity down, shifting stress "from metabolic intensity to cumulative mechanical load"; "stacking back-to-back long runs or several medium-long runs in succession" — [How to Build Fatigue Resistance for Ultras](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/fatigue-resistance-ultras-molly-seidel-training/)
- [General endurance] Mini-blocks: within a 7–10-day microcycle, "two to three higher-volume or key aerobic sessions, followed by one to two days of reduced volume or recovery running"; "the athlete learns to run efficiently, fuel appropriately, and manage effort while carrying residual fatigue. This repeated exposure is a key driver of durability"; one anchor session (long run / extended steady effort), surrounding days add volume not intensity — [How to Build Fatigue Resistance for Ultras](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/fatigue-resistance-ultras-molly-seidel-training/)
- [General endurance] Execution factors that compound: "fueling tolerance, hydration strategy, pacing discipline… Downhill running… treated as a skill rather than an opportunity to push intensity" — [How to Build Fatigue Resistance for Ultras](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/fatigue-resistance-ultras-molly-seidel-training/)
- [General endurance] Human efficiency framing: Gross efficiency = biochemical + mechanical efficiency; a heat block that yields "more watts for the same heart rate and core temperature" is an efficiency gain; "There's no speed without power and no power without calories" — [Understanding Human Efficiency](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/human-efficiency-impact-triathlon-achievement/)

**Outside the Science category (TrainingPeaks, secondary)**
- [General endurance] Friel's decoupling bands: "< 5%: strong aerobic endurance at that intensity; 5–10%: moderate endurance limitations or fatigue; > 10%: the effort was likely above aerobic threshold or the athlete lacks the endurance"; cycling durability benchmark "two to four hours at aerobic threshold (AeT)", running "one to two hours at AeT"; "a half-distance triathlon bike leg… around 2.5 hours, a 2.5-hour steady AeT ride is a logical target"; sprint/Olympic bike → 2 h AeT is sufficient; Ironman bike → 4 h is the upper limit — [Aerobic Decoupling and Heart Rate Drift Explained (Friel)](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/aerobic-endurance-and-decoupling/)
- [General endurance] Decoupling is EF(first half) vs EF(second half); "you are looking for a decoupling of 5% or less over the course of your target duration"; if > 5% on a low-VI long session, first check temperature/hydration, then add Zone 2 — [Intro to TP Metrics Part Two](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/an-introduction-to-more-trainingpeaks-metrics-part-two/)

### Inferences
- An assistant can implement Rowe's protocol directly from FIT data: find the best 5-min (or race-relevant) power in the first 20 min of long rides and again after cumulative work ≥ 2,000 kJ (or ≥ 1,000 kJ per his TP workflow), and report the % drop; a 10% drop at 2,000 kJ is the article's illustrative example, not a benchmark of "good" or "bad".
- For runs, the analogous fatigue signal set from the category is: Pa:HR decoupling on the steady portion (target ≤ 5%), second-half vs first-half power/pace/Running Effectiveness and power:GCT ratio, and HR-pegged-while-pace-falls as the signature of going out too hard.
- Training levers the assistant can recommend for durability, in the category's priority: (1) years of consistent volume, (2) lots of sub-LT1 volume with long sessions, (3) back-to-back long days / mini-blocks with real recovery after, (4) race-effort Z3 segments inside long runs, (5) gut-training to 30–120 g/h carbs.

### Gaps
- No article gives durability thresholds for triathletes (e.g., "acceptable % power loss after N kJ for a 70.3"), nor the kJ demand of a full or half-distance bike leg.
- No Science-category article quantifies long-ride or long-run durations for triathlon (e.g., "ride X hours at Y% FTP"); the only duration benchmarks are Friel's (outside the category).

## Key question 3 — What do they say about pacing hilly and windy courses and about heat-adjusted targets?

### Takeaway
The category's pacing physics is explicit: drag rises with speed squared, so constant power is only optimal on a flat, windless road; ride harder on climbs and "recover" on descents (doubling power on a descent adds only ~40% speed), and use tools like Best Bike Split. For heat, the single most important adjustment is to lower target effort and pace by RPE/HR; the CORE Heat Strain Index gives numeric decoupling thresholds (HSI ≥ 3.0 → substantial HR/power decoupling; ≤ 5% decoupling is the practical ceiling for long events).

### Cited Findings

**Hills, wind, aerodynamics**
- [Triathlon-specific] "Apps and websites such as BestBikeSplit have shown us that riding with constant power is not usually the fastest way to ride a course unless it is pan-flat with no wind… aerodynamic resistance increases with the square of the speed… From 20kph on the climb to 80kph on a descent, drag force will increase 16 times!" — [Make Big Gains Race Planning a Triathlon Using Science](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/race-planning-triathlon-using-science/)
- [Triathlon-specific] "If you pedal double the power on the descent, your speed will only increase about 40%, which is hardly a good return on investment. Whereas, if you increase your effort on the ascent, the boost in speed has a higher factor on overall speed… doing more watts on the climb and 'recovering' on the descent is not only technically easier but faster, too." Pedal "out of corners" and to hold speed on descents — [Race Planning a Triathlon Using Science](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/race-planning-triathlon-using-science/)
- [Triathlon-specific] Hilly-course nutrition weight: Gustav Iden (2019 70.3 Worlds, Nice) lost his bottles at the base of the climb and refuelled at the top; "the weight penalty for hauling your nutrition is worth taking if the bike course is relatively flat"; on the run, using on-course nutrition can save "up to 300g" (9–12 gels for 80 g/h over 3 h) — [Race Planning a Triathlon Using Science](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/race-planning-triathlon-using-science/)
- [Triathlon-specific] Run split variance: "Professional run splits will differ from course to course in long-distance races by up to 15 minutes, and this time gap is exacerbated by amateurs" — driven by "course length, hills or weather and the difficulty of the bike course" — [Race Planning a Triathlon Using Science](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/race-planning-triathlon-using-science/)
- [General endurance] "Riding at exactly the same power is only the most efficient use of your energy on a completely flat, windless, perfectly surfaced road… how long will the race take me… and what features will I have to navigate (think hills, wind and road surface primarily)"; "the longer you ride, the bigger the time differences these aero gains will make"; "there's no point in training for six months to gain an extra 30W of fitness if you waste 30W with a dirty bike and sticky bearings" — [Understanding the Physics of Cycling](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/the-physics-of-cycling/)
- [General endurance] Toy model P = ½ρu³·CdA + 30 W (20 W rolling + 10 W drivetrain); air density term explains why altitude helps despite power loss — [Aerodynamics or Power?](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/whats-important-aerodynamics-power/)
- [General endurance] Drafting: "the air resistance… in a big peloton may decrease 5 to 10 percent compared to… a solo rider… a rider in the middle of the back of the peloton [at 54 kph] only encounters air resistance equivalent to 12 kph" (Blocken) — [Critical Periods](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/should-you-be-evaluating-your-cyclists-using-critical-periods/); long-course pro triathlon's "legal draft distance of 12m can provide significant energy savings" — [Speed v. Endurance](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/speed-v-endurance/)
- [General endurance] Running in wind: "roughly 2% of a marathon runner's energy goes to overcoming air resistance. This could convert to up to three and a half minutes for a three hour marathon runner… running in a pack is the best way to avoid the wind"; cadence 170–200 spm "optimal for most runners" — [Physics of Running](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/physics-running-triathlon-coaches/)
- [General endurance] On hilly run courses ("Boston Marathon or Ironman St. George run course"), "speed is less helpful in pacing an effort" — use HR or run power — [Is Coaching With Running Power Better?](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/coaching-running-power-better/); run power lets an athlete hold an "easy zone" on hills and recover intensity after tight turns — [How To Use Run Power For Race-Specific Training](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/how-to-use-run-power-for-race-specific-training/)

**Heat**
- [General endurance] "The single most important adjustment athletes should make racing in heat is to lower the target effort… Blindly adhering to pacing goals is a recipe for disaster. Instead, athletes should rely more heavily on rated perceived exertion and heart rate within the race, both of which will be higher than on mild days"; "The likelihood of setting a personal best on a hot day is small" — [How to Train and Race in Hot Conditions](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/how-to-train-and-race-in-hot-conditions/)
- [General endurance] Acclimation: "about 10 to 14 days of training in hot weather"; start with 30-min easy sessions, +~10 min/day; sauna 10–15 sessions of 20–30 min post-exercise; race hydration 16–32 oz/h and 600–1,200 mg sodium/h; short races (< 90 min) at higher intensity absorb fluid worse, so drink slightly less; "it is well worth slowing down at aid stations" for ice/sponges — [How to Train and Race in Hot Conditions](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/how-to-train-and-race-in-hot-conditions/)
- [General endurance] Heat-training protocol (Hutchinson): normal sessions + sauna/hot tub 20–30 min, 3×/week for two weeks, core ~101 °F; maintain 1–2 heat sessions/week if the block ends > 2 weeks before the race; heat-acclimatised runners "still got a lot faster even when testing in cooler conditions" (Minson 2010) — [Everything Coaches Need to Know About Heat Training](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/everything-coaches-need-to-know-about-heat-training/)
- [General endurance] CORE Heat Strain Index zones: HSI 0–0.9 no strain; 1.0–2.9 "heart rate is starting to decouple from power/pace"; 3.0–6.9 "heart rate has substantially decoupled… Performance and exercise capacity are substantially reduced"; ≥ 7.0 extreme. Example 6:30 ride: HSI 3.8 after an hour of climbing gave ~8% power:HR decoupling in a well-adapted athlete. Threshold test: hold warm-up HR, cut power as HSI rises, stop at −15–20% power; "For longer events, most consider 5% the maximum amount of decoupling for optimal performance." Heat sessions need core > 38.0 °C and skin > 35 °C — [Analyze 360](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/see-and-analyze-every-metric/)
- [General endurance] Sweat/sodium: sodium concentration rises with sweat rate; "two sessions with the same average power can have very different sodium losses if the '3-minute slope' of sweat rate is higher due to heat, humidity, or a harder surging profile" — [Analyze 360](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/see-and-analyze-every-metric/)

### Inferences
- A pacing rule consistent with the category: on hills, allow power above target on climbs (bounded by the athlete's durability and the VI ≤ 1.05 whole-leg ceiling) and drop power on descents once speed is high (drag ∝ v²; > ~50 km/h pedalling returns little); constant power is the correct model only for flat, calm courses. The category does not quantify how much above target to climb — that is what Best Bike Split is recommended for.
- For heat, the category supports HR/RPE-based rather than power/pace-based targets and gives no % power reduction per °C; a diagnosing assistant can, however, flag heat as the likely cause when a race file shows decoupling > 5% with HSI/temperature high, versus fitness/pacing when conditions were mild.
- Run-course adjustment: budget up to ~15 min variance in pro long-course run splits (more for amateurs) for course, weather and bike difficulty when predicting splits.

### Gaps
- No Science-category article quantifies a heat-adjusted power or pace target (e.g., "reduce IF by X per °C or per humidity band").
- No article gives a numeric "climb at X% above / descend at Y% below target" rule, or wind-specific pacing numbers; these are deferred to Best Bike Split.

## Key question 4 — What taper length and TSB do they recommend, and how do they analyse a race afterwards?

### Takeaway
The Science category contains no numeric taper length or race-day TSB: its taper article is about psychology (rename it the "peaking phase", keep compliance green), and the closest quantitative statements are "avoid TSB below −30, ramp rate 5–7 CTL points/week" (Simmons) and a CTL-based race-prediction heuristic (race TSS ≈ peak CTL, verified in 20 of 25 marathoners). The +15 to +25 race-day Form target is Joe Friel's, published outside the category. Post-race analysis in the category = EF, IF, VI (Couzens) plus first-half/second-half decoupling and power-based fatigue markers.

### Cited Findings

**Taper / race-week load (Science category)**
- [General endurance] "There is no question that coaches need to reduce fatigue leading into an event to ensure peak physiological performance"; taper-lovers skip sessions and turn a "green machine" TP calendar "into a rainbow", taper-dreaders add unplanned efforts ("I feel so unfit, I'll just throw in a few efforts"); reframe as the "peaking phase" and keep compliance green — no durations or TSB numbers given — [Rethinking the Taper Phase](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/rethinking-the-taper-phase/)
- [General endurance] Load guardrails: "generally avoid a TSB of greater than -30, keep ramp rate to five to seven points of CTL, and minimize overloading in any direction more than 10%"; Target CTL = "the highest volume of accumulated training load the athlete will achieve before starting the taper" — [How To Design An Endurance Athlete's Total Training Load](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/designing-an-athletes-total-load/)
- [General endurance] Race prediction / taper adequacy from CTL: "a proper and appropriate taper for an athlete is when the 24 hour TSS load of the athlete doesn't push their response or predicted CTL beyond the highest CTL they achieved in training… tested approximately 25 athletes… consistent in predicting marathon times in 20 of the 25 cases… if an athlete… hits a CTL of 63 — they should be able to perform an effort that produces enough TSS load to produce a rise of 63"; he builds the predicted race in the Workout Builder at 0.91–0.95 of threshold pace and uses it "four to seven weeks out to guide our more critical workouts" — [3 Things Your Athletes' Running Data Could Be Telling You](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/athletes-running-data/)
- [General endurance] Taper physiology check via HRV: "higher HRV during taper (RCV, compared to baseline BSL) has been associated with improved performance – meaning, the training has worked"; functionally overreached runners' performance "improved only slightly at the end of recovery"; record wellness when "TSB and the acute chronic training load ratio get worse" — [Functional Overreaching vs. Excess Fatigue](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/the-fine-line-between-functional-training-and-overreaching/)
- [General endurance] Heat block timing relative to race: maintain 1–2 heat sessions/week if the block ends > 2 weeks out — [Everything Coaches Need to Know About Heat Training](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/everything-coaches-need-to-know-about-heat-training/)
- [General endurance] Sleep in the "two to four weeks leading up to an event" as the aerobic-capacity lever — [3 Unconventional Ways to Build Aerobic Capacity](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/coaching-build-aerobic-capacity/)

**Post-race analysis (Science category)**
- [Triathlon-specific] Race-file triage = EF (fitness), IF (specific endurance), VI (pacing evenness); "descriptive analytics of a good athlete executing a good strategy become prescriptive analytics in formulating an optimal training plan" — [3 Key Metrics](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/3-key-metrics-of-effective-data-analysis-for-coaches/)
- [General endurance] Post-race/post-season: use the fitness history chart (Peak Pace, Pace by Distance, HR) — "Sixty-minute speed month-to-month… for many marathoners, and 20-minute speed… for… the 10K"; ask "Did you do many runs or hard intervals over 20 or 60 minutes? Were conditions suitable?" — [3 Things Your Athletes' Running Data](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/athletes-running-data/)
- [General endurance] First-half vs second-half comparison of power, speed, Running Effectiveness, power:GCT, LSS, GCT, vertical oscillation to separate "legs crapping out" (RPE) from real output loss — [Using WKO4 to Analyze the Struggle](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/using-wko4-to-analyze-the-struggle-in-a-run-workout/)
- [General endurance] Single-ride MMP curve vs 90-day curve shows the nature of a race: curve close to 90-day bests at 40–60 min but not short durations = "a sustained hard effort"; high at 10–30 min plus sprint = virtual-race pattern — [All About the Mean Max Power Curve](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/all-about-the-mean-max-power-curve/)
- [General endurance] Layer heat (HSI, core/skin temp), sweat and breathing on power/HR to explain "why power, pace, and heart rate drift even when a workout looks the same on paper"; create custom laps for any segment — [Analyze 360](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/see-and-analyze-every-metric/)
- [General endurance] Goal setting for the next race: finishers cluster just under round numbers (1.4× more likely to finish 3:59 than 4:01; 300,324 finished in the 3 min before 4:00 vs 212,477 in the 3 min after); "stretch goals by five minutes, or for longer races such as ultra marathons or full-length IRONMAN events, stretch goals by two to five percent" — [What Researchers Found After Studying 10 Million Marathon Finishers](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/what-researchers-found-after-studying-10-million-marathon-finishers/)
- [Triathlon-specific] Swim analysis: compare "% difference from the fastest swimmer" and age-group rank across races because "elapsed times alone in open water swimming can be meaningless, and GPS data is notoriously unreliable" — [How to Use Data Science for Analyzing Endurance Athletes](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/how-data-science-analyzing-endurance-athletes/)

**Outside the Science category (TrainingPeaks, secondary)**
- [General endurance] "When I'm tapering and peaking athletes for A-priority races I like to have their Form at around plus 15 to plus 25 on race day… there are athletes who perform best when their Form is just barely positive, around plus 5 to plus 10"; Form below −30 "greatly increases your risk of injury or illness"; "north of plus 25 your training is much too easy"; −10 to +10 for two weeks or more = stagnant training; Form value shown is for tomorrow — [Applying the Numbers Part 3 (Friel)](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/learn/articles/applying-the-numbers-part-3-training-stress-balance/)
- [Triathlon-specific] Post-race example: IF 0.82 / VI 1.02 at 70.3 Worlds but a hard final 7.5 km left the quads "very fatigued" and cost the run — evidence that whole-leg IF/VI can look correct while a late surge still damages the run — [Taking Risks](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/taking-risks-ironman-70-3-world-championships-bike-pacing/)

### Inferences
- Race-prediction rule (from Simmons, general endurance): a marathon-type effort should not require more TSS than the athlete's peak CTL; for triathlon the analogous check would be bike+run race TSS vs peak CTL, but the category provides no triathlon validation.
- Taper guardrails an assistant can enforce from the category alone: never drive TSB below −30, ramp ≤ 5–7 CTL/week in the build, avoid > 10% overload, keep race-week compliance close to plan (no added intensity, no skipped sessions); race-day Form +15 to +25 (or +5 to +10 for some) must be cited to Friel outside the category.
- A post-race diagnostic flow consistent with all sources: (1) whole-leg IF and VI vs target; (2) segment-level: last-10–20% power vs first-part power, decoupling per steady segment (> 5% = fatigue/heat/fuel/going out too hard); (3) run: HR-pegged-pace-falling pattern, power vs pace divergence (economy loss), Running Effectiveness / power:GCT drop; (4) environmental overlay (temperature, HSI) to separate heat from fitness; (5) check late-bike surges even when whole-leg VI ≤ 1.05.

### Gaps
- No Science-category article gives taper length (days/weeks) by race distance, race-week TSS, or race-day TSB/Form targets.
- No article gives an expected run-split model (e.g., open-marathon pace × factor) for running off the bike, nor walking-share or pace-fade benchmarks for triathlon runs.
- No article gives swim pacing targets (e.g., % of CSS) — swim content in the category is limited to stroke-rate/stroke-length adaptation in chop (shorten stroke ~10%, raise rate ~10% to offset waves), goggle choice, and the 9-reasons OW article's test sets (e.g., 4×400/4×300/4×200/2×100 to estimate an IRONMAN-length swim time) — [Race Planning a Triathlon Using Science](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/race-planning-triathlon-using-science/), [9 Reasons Why Pool Speed May Not Translate to Open Water](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/9-reasons-why-pool-speed-may-not-translate-to-openwater/).
- I found no Science-category article on brick sessions or run-off-the-bike prediction beyond the Hatzis power observation; "How to Run Faster in an Olympic-Distance Triathlon" (Brodie) is about breathing drills, hill sprints and strength, with no pacing numbers — https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach-blog/how-to-run-faster-in-an-olympic-distance-triathlon/.
